SARC 1.5 tuning overview

Compared with unmodified ExecuTorch 1.5 (Vulkan backend, no cooperative matrix instructions), 2048-token prefill is 2.8 to 7.9 times faster on the seven GPUs with published figures (geometric mean of six cells: the three models 1B, 3B and 8B, and the two quantization schemes 4w and 8da4w). The large number on each card below is this speed-up; the card's second line splits it into two parts: what the earlier configuration had already achieved (the September SARC release; for the RX 7600 and the RX 7900 XTX the device's own parent commit, and the card says so), and the percentage that this round of tuning added on top of it. Figures 1 to 4 are about the second part: which step the gain over the earlier configuration came from, which techniques were used where, where the time was saved, and which work did not pay. For the M51, at the device owner's request, only qualitative conclusions are listed. Figure 8 separately compares the tuned kernels of five of the devices with reference shaders that only perform the matrix multiply-add; its numbers come from the roof study of igpu-roofline.

Where the remaining headroom is

The fused kernel is adopted on six devices, and the M51 uses the two-pass form; the fused-kernel result on the RX 7900 XTX was measured under the AMDVLK driver, with the subgroup width unverified; on 2026-10-09 the owner decided not to re-measure under RADV, and the result is kept as it is. On the B580, the B70 and the Orin attention is down to 5 % to 14 % of prefill dispatch time, and most of the remaining time is in the linear kernels. The reference measurements of 2026-10-09 (figure 8) put the 8da4w kernel at 91 % to 96 % of the rate of a reference shader fed at the same reuse on the B580 and at 92 % to 98 % on the B70 (per model), and at 58 % of the 32 ops/B reference row on the Orin, whose kernel has a reuse of 42.7. That reference is a yardstick, not a ceiling (it runs at a different subgroup width on Intel, some of its rows are single runs, and the kernel reads above it on some shapes), so these figures do not say how much speed remains. That low reuse is what keeps these kernels far from the register roof is a hypothesis consistent with the reference rows; it has not been tested by changing reuse in a real kernel. Raising reuse would mean performing more matrix multiply-adds for each operand loaded, which is limited by the number of registers (the Orin's 8da4w kernel already uses 209 registers per thread, and 4w uses 128). On 2026-10-09 the owner decided not to start a third round now. On the Orin the fused kernel reaches only 16 % to 20 % of the matrix roof; going faster needs a new kernel that stages K and V through shared memory. On AMD discrete GPUs the HIP backend of llama.cpp has not been measured yet, so the lead there holds for llama.cpp Vulkan Q4_0 only.

Process artifacts

A/A calibration and the clock floor, a complete gate for every candidate before it is adopted, the reference-error rule, the stop rule of two consecutive candidates below 2 %, coordinator holds, independent review. These are part of the playbook and are not in the figures.

Known defect: pipelines lack the full-subgroups flag

The Vulkan specification requires compute pipelines of this kind, which depend on full subgroups, to be created with the "full subgroups" flag. None of the cooperative matrix pipelines on the branches carries it, including the released ones and those of the fused kernels. On 2026-10-09 the owner decided to record this as a known defect and to fix it once, in the release zone, before any merge. What is known: every gate passes on the drivers used, and both arms of every comparison between SARC configurations on this page (parent and candidate, and the released kernels against the tuned ones) create their pipelines the same way, without the flag, so those comparisons are like for like. In the comparisons against unmodified ExecuTorch and against llama.cpp only the SARC side has the defect. What is not known: the effect of creating the pipelines with the flag has not been measured. After the fix every device is to be gated and timed again. The RX 7900 XTX's campaign additionally found that some kernels declare more workgroup shared memory than the limit AMDVLK reports, among them one released 4w kernel. The driver accepts these pipelines and every gate passes; the largest overrun is in the parent's kernel table, so both arms of that campaign's comparisons have it. Whether staying within the reported limit changes the timing has not been measured. This item will be handled before merging, together with the full-subgroups flag (F1).

Decode slightly slower, cause not investigated

On the B70, with the fused node, decode (generating tokens one by one after prefill) is 0 to 2.5 % slower than the parent configuration; on the B580 all six cells are also slightly below the parent configuration (0.985 to 0.998). The fused kernel does not handle decode calls, and where the slowdown occurs has not been located yet. Decode is not a gate item; it is recorded here so that it is not missed at promotion. The owner decided on 2026-10-09 that the investigation is scheduled before promotion and is not done now.

The Orin's clock

The Orin earlier had two sets of roof figures (matrix fp16 about 16.0 and 9.7 TFLOP/s). The difference comes from the GPU clock: the set of 2026-09-26 was measured with the clock locked at 1020 MHz; the device was rebooted afterwards, and in the 15 W power mode the clock limit is 612 MHz. All Orin figures on this page (tok/s, kernel rates, the roofs of figure 8) are for the 15 W state. The higher power mode was not measured; on 2026-10-09 the owner decided not to measure it.
